# Indoor Air Quality & Ventilation Guide

## Winter Inversion & Safe Ventilation
In Indian urban centers during winter, thermal inversion traps particulate matter close to the ground. 
Between 6:00 AM and 10:00 AM, cooler ground temperatures keep pollutants concentrated at street and window level. 

- **When to ventilate:** The safest window for cross-ventilation is between **1:00 PM and 4:00 PM**, when solar radiation heats the ground, breaking the inversion layer and allowing vertical dispersion of pollutants.
- **When to seal:** Keep windows and balcony doors shut from 6:00 PM until 11:00 AM next morning.
- **Micro-climate check:** If live outdoor AQI exceeds 300 (Very Poor), avoid cross-ventilation entirely; rely on mechanical filtration.

## Mechanical Filtration: Commercial HEPA vs. DIY Corsi-Rosenthal Box
- **Commercial HEPA Purifier:** Excellent for sealed bedrooms. Ensure the Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR) matches room size (CADR in CFM should be at least 2/3 of room square footage). Cost: ₹10,000 to ₹28,000 INR.
- **DIY Corsi-Rosenthal (C-R) Box:** Constructed using a standard 20-inch box fan and four MERV-13 filters taped in a cube with a cardboard shroud. Provides high CADR (350–450 CFM) at roughly one-third the cost (₹3,000–₹4,000 INR). Highly recommended for large living rooms, classrooms, and community halls.

## Cooking Emissions & Indoor Sources
Cooking is a major contributor to indoor PM2.5 and nitrogen dioxide (NO2):
- LPG combustion and high-heat oil frying produce peaks of PM2.5 exceeding 400 µg/m³.
- Always run kitchen exhaust or chimney with a dedicated duct during and 15 minutes after cooking.
- Switching to induction cooktops eliminates indoor NO2 and carbon monoxide.
- Avoid incense sticks (agarbatti) and dhoop; burning 2 agarbattis in a closed room raises PM2.5 to hazardous levels for over 4 hours.

## Cleaning Practices
- Never dry sweep; sweeping resuspends PM10 and settled PM2.5 into breathing air.
- Use damp microfiber cloths and wet mopping with plain water or mild eco-friendly cleaners.
- Beware the houseplant myth: Common indoor plants (like snake plants or money plants) do not remove PM2.5 in real-world ventilated homes. Rely on certified physical filters.
