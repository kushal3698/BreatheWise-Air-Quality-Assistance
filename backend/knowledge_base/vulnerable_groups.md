# Vulnerable Populations: Children, Seniors, and Respiratory Conditions

## Children & Pediatric Lung Health
- Children inhale more air per kilogram of body weight than adults (higher metabolic rate and respiration).
- Their lungs and immune defenses are actively developing until young adulthood (ages 8 to 18).
- Prolonged exposure to PM2.5 and NO2 causes permanent deficits in forced expiratory volume (FEV1), increases lifetime asthma risk, and triggers frequent acute respiratory infections.
- **Action plan:** When AQI exceeds 200, children should not participate in outdoor sports, athletic meets, or outdoor physical education.

## Elderly & Cardiovascular Strain
- In seniors, acute PM2.5 spikes provoke systemic inflammation, autonomic nervous system imbalance, and plaque vulnerability.
- Hospital admissions for myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, and congestive heart failure rise significantly within 24 to 72 hours of severe smog episodes.
- **Action plan:** Morning walks should be avoided when winter temperatures drop and AQI exceeds 200. Substitute with indoor walking inside well-ventilated or air-purified rooms.

## Asthma, COPD & Allergies
- Air pollution triggers airway hyperresponsiveness, bronchospasm, and mucus hypersecretion.
- Patients must keep reliever inhalers (e.g. Salbutamol / Levosalbutamol) within immediate reach.
- Adherence to maintenance controller inhalers (inhaled corticosteroids) is vital during high pollution seasons.
- Peak flow meter monitoring can provide an early warning of airway narrowing before severe symptoms manifest.

## Pregnancy & Maternal Health
- Ambient particulate exposure can cross the placental barrier, leading to placental inflammation.
- Strongly associated with increased risk of low birth weight, pre-term delivery, and gestational hypertension.
- Expectant mothers should minimize outdoor exposure during Poor to Severe AQI episodes and ensure indoor living spaces maintain PM2.5 below 35 µg/m³.
