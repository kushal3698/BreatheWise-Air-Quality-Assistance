# Physical Activity and Air Pollution Guidance

## Inhalation Dynamics During Exercise
During moderate-to-vigorous exercise:
- Minute ventilation volume increases from 6–8 liters/minute at rest to **40–80 liters/minute**.
- Shift from nasal to oral breathing bypasses natural nasal filtration (nasal hairs and turbinates), allowing fine and ultrafine particles (PM2.5 and PM0.1) to penetrate deep into pulmonary alveoli and enter the bloodstream.
- Inhaling high levels of PM2.5 during cardio causes acute bronchial inflammation, vascular endothelial dysfunction, and elevated blood pressure.

## AQI Thresholds for Outdoor Physical Activity
Based on India Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) bands:
- **AQI 0–50 (Good):** All outdoor physical activities and running are safe.
- **AQI 51–100 (Satisfactory):** Generally safe for healthy adults. Sensitive individuals (asthma, COPD) should monitor symptoms.
- **AQI 101–200 (Moderate):** Shift intense endurance training (half-marathons, cycling) away from major traffic corridors. Elderly and children should avoid prolonged strenuous workouts.
- **AQI 201–300 (Poor):** Cancel outdoor runs and strenuous cardio. Move workouts indoors.
- **AQI 301–400 (Very Poor):** Strict indoor exercise only. Keep indoor air filtered. Do not jog outdoors even with a mask, as breathing resistance causes hyperventilation.
- **AQI 401–500 (Severe):** Limit all strenuous physical activity indoors and outdoors. Favor gentle stretching, restorative yoga, and meditation.

## Safer Daily Windows for Movement
- Early mornings (5:00 AM – 8:30 AM) have the highest ground-level pollution due to cold air trapping emissions.
- Mid-afternoon (1:00 PM – 4:00 PM) generally presents lower particulate concentrations.
- If walking outdoors is necessary, choose shaded residential parks far from arterial roads, wear a well-fitted N95 mask, and keep duration under 25 minutes.
